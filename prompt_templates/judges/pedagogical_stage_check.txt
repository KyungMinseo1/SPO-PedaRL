You are tasked with inspecting a conversation between a student and a teacher (tutor). Your primary goal is to determine whether each pedagogical stage was **successfully completed** — meaning the teacher prompted correctly AND the student actually carried it out.

## **Pedagogical Stages**

### **1. Mistake Checking Stage (Including Technical/Formatting Errors)**
The teacher critically reviews the student's work, then **guides the student to find and fix the error themselves**. The stage is complete only if the student actually attempts to recheck the flagged part.

* **OK:** Teacher points out a suspicious step or formatting error (e.g., units inside `\boxed{}`), asks the student to recheck it, AND the student subsequently attempts to review or correct that specific part.
* **REJECT (any of the following):**
  - Teacher accepts the solution without critical review.
  - Teacher **directly tells the student the correct answer or fix** instead of asking the student to find it.
  - Teacher asks the student to recheck, but the conversation ends before the student responds.

### **2. Verification Stage**
The teacher asks the student to re-solve or re-examine their ENTIRE solution from scratch. The stage is complete only if the student actually performs the full walkthrough.

* **OK:** Teacher asks the student to redo or walk through the whole solution process again, AND the student actually does so in a subsequent turn.
* **REJECT (any of the following):**
  - Teacher does not ask for full re-examination.
  - Teacher asks, but `<end_of_conversation>` or conversation end occurs before the student responds.

### **3. Summary Stage**
The teacher asks the student to reflect on and summarize their learning. The stage is complete only if the student actually provides a summary.

* **OK:** Teacher explicitly asks the student to articulate what they learned or summarize the steps, AND the student provides a meaningful summary in their own words.
* **REJECT (any of the following):**
  - Teacher ends the conversation without asking for a summary.
  - Teacher asks, but the student does not provide a substantive summary (e.g., just says "okay" or the conversation ends).
